Body
Recomp

Three splits for intermediate lifters in a caloric deficit — designed to preserve muscle, drive fat loss, and progress week over week. Pick the one that fits your recovery and schedule.

01

Push / Pull

Legs squeezed onto push days

Lower body split across two "push + legs" days — quads on Monday, posterior chain on Wednesday. Every leg session lives inside your Mon–Thu window, and one workload per body part per week keeps recovery honest during a cut.

Progression Rules

  • Compounds (bench, squat, OHP, deadlift, row): add 2.5 kg when you hit the top of the rep range on all sets with clean form.
  • Isolations: add reps first, then add weight once you've held the top of the range for two sessions.
  • Log every session — RPE, weight, reps. Progressive overload during a cut is guesswork without a log.

Cut-Phase Notes

  • Rest periods are non-negotiable on the compounds — strength retention matters more than sweating through a session.
  • If a compound feels 1–2 reps weaker two sessions in a row, check sleep, protein, and calories before blaming programming.
  • Optional finisher (2×/week max): 8–10 min incline walk after training. Skip intervals — recovery is already stressed.

Antagonist Pairs

Supersets · Highest metabolic demand

Progression Rules

  • Compounds: add 2.5 kg once you hit the top of the rep range on all sets with clean form.
  • Isolations: add reps first, then weight.
  • Superset pacing: if your rep drop-off between A and B is huge (>3 reps), rest 30s between them instead of 15s.

Cut-Phase Notes

  • Highest metabolic demand of the three splits — best if you also want the "cardio" effect baked into lifting.
  • Day 4 chest/back feels like a repeat of Day 1 by design — different angles and rep tempo drive extra hypertrophy stimulus.
  • Skip separate cardio days entirely on this split. The supersets are the conditioning.
  • If heart rate spikes too high mid-superset (can't finish exercise B cleanly), extend rest between A and B — never sacrifice compound quality.
