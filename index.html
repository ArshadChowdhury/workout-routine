Why Antagonist Supersets on Wed

  • Less perceived soreness during the session — when you work chest, the back rests and takes light blood flow. Then the reverse. Each muscle feels less beaten up because it gets active recovery between its sets.
  • Time efficient — Wed drops from ~95 min to ~70 min without losing volume. Rest for one muscle = work for the other.
  • Same load, same reps — antagonist pairing doesn't reduce strength like same-muscle supersets do. You can still lift heavy in 1A and 2A.
  • Better pump — alternating opposing muscles increases blood flow to the whole region, so the whole upper body feels worked as a system, not a checklist.
  • Wed hits everything from Sat + Sun again — chest, back (3 angles), shoulders (front + side + rear), biceps, triceps, traps, core. Second exposure for all major upper-body muscles.

Muscle Coverage

  • Chest: Sat (Smith/BB Bench, Incline DB, Pec Deck) + Wed (Incline BB, Cable Fly) = 2x
  • Shoulders (front + all heads): Sat (Arnold Press) + Wed (Standing OHP) = 2x
  • Shoulders (side): Sat (Lateral Raise) + Wed (Lateral Raise) = 2x
  • Rear Delts + Rotator Cuff: Sun (Face Pull) + Wed (Face Pull) = 2x
  • Triceps: Sat (Close-Grip, Rope Ext) + Wed (Pushdown) = 2x
  • Back: Sun (Lat Pulldown, BB Row, CSR) + Wed (Lat Pulldown, BB Row, CSR) = 2x with 6 total row/pulldown exposures
  • Traps: Sun (Shrug) + Wed (Shrug) = 2x
  • Biceps (long head/outer): Sun (Incline DB Curl, Bayesian Cable Curl) + Wed (Cable Curl) = 3 long-head exposures — fixes the inner-head dominance from preacher-heavy training
  • Brachialis + Forearms: Sun (Hammer Curl) = 1x direct — pushes biceps up as it grows, adds arm width from the side view
  • Quads: Tue (Squat, Leg Press, Leg Extension) = 1x with three exposures
  • Hamstrings: Tue (RDL, Lying Leg Curl) = 1x with concentrated volume
  • Glutes: Tue (Squat, RDL, Pull-Through) = 1x with concentrated volume

  • Abs: Sat + Sun + Tue + Wed = 4x with variety

Superset Execution Rules

  • Set up both stations before starting — load the bench AND the row bar before your first set. Nothing kills the flow like walking across the gym to grab plates mid-superset.
  • Rest 30s between A → B, 90s between full rounds. If you rest longer, it stops being a superset — it just becomes two separate exercises.
  • Match your weights to Sat/Sun — don't try to PR the compounds on Wed. Use the same working loads from your equivalent lifts earlier in the week.
  • If the gym is busy and you can't hold two stations, run 1A + 1B → then 2A + 2B → etc. as circuits. Slightly less efficient but still antagonist pairing.

Progression Rules

  • Compounds (Smith/BB Bench, Arnold, OHP, RDL, BB Row, Lat Pulldown, Squat): add 2.5 kg when you hit the top of the rep range on all working sets with clean form.
  • Machines & isolations: add reps first — once you hold the top of the range for 2 sessions, then bump the weight one increment.
  • Log every session — weight × reps × RPE. Progressive overload in a deficit is a game of small weekly wins.

Fixing the Biceps Imbalance

  • The problem with preacher-heavy training: the preacher pad puts your elbow in front of the body, which shortens the long head of the biceps (the outer/lateral head). Result: the short head (inner) gets hammered every set while the long head barely stretches — inner grows, outer stays flat, arm looks narrow from the front.
  • The fix — train with elbows behind or at the body so the long head is fully stretched. Incline DB Curl and Bayesian Cable Curl both do this; they're arguably the two best long-head builders that exist.
  • Incline DB Curl is the classic king — full stretch at the bottom, hard eccentric. Expect to use much less weight than standing curls (that stretch is brutal).
  • Bayesian Cable Curl keeps constant tension throughout the entire ROM, which the DB version loses at the top. Best done one arm at a time so you can really focus.
  • Hammer Curl doesn't hit the biceps directly, but the brachialis sits underneath the biceps — as it grows, it pushes the biceps up and out. You'll see arm width increase from the side view.
  • Give it 8–12 weeks before you judge — muscle growth pattern shifts don't show up week 1. Track a mirror photo now for comparison.

Cut-Phase Notes

  • Total volume: ~36 exercises/week (8 + 9 + 8 + 11). High but well distributed with Wed's superset structure keeping the session compact.
  • Sat + Sun back-to-back — respect the intra-workout rest periods. Sun's back work will feel heavier because of Sat's shoulder fatigue; that's normal.
  • Knee still recovering? Sub Leg Extension for Bulgarian Split Squat (3 × 10/leg, moderate depth). Leg extension is one of the worst exercises for patellofemoral pain.
  • If Wed supersets still leave you smoked, drop the accessory block (Shrug + Plank) first — the 5 supersets alone hit everything critical.
  • Optional finisher (max 2×/week): 8–10 min incline walk after training. Only after Sat or Sun, never after Wed.

Substitutions

  • Smith vs Barbell Bench — either works on Sat. Alternate week to week if you want both stimuli.
  • Arnold Press too tricky? Fall back to Seated DB Shoulder Press (10 kg each).
  • Goblet Squat instead of Back Squat? Sub in during weeks 1–4 or if knee is still healing. Progress to back squat when knee is 100%.
  • Knee still sore on Tue? Skip Back Squat AND Leg Extension. Do Leg Press twice + Bulgarian Split Squat 3×10/leg.
  • Lower back bothered by RDL? Swap for Barbell Hip Thrust or Cable Pull-Through — both hit glutes/hams without spinal load.
  • Wed gym too crowded for supersets? Do everything as straight sets. Session becomes ~90 min but volume is identical.
  • No T-bar row? Chest-supported DB row on incline bench works.
  • No Cable Fly setup? DB Fly on flat or incline bench works, though slightly more shoulder stress.
  • Bayesian Cable Curl station busy? Do a second set of Incline DB Curls in its place — you'll get long-head work either way. Or standing DB curl with elbows pulled slightly back (10–15° behind torso) mimics the same stretch angle.
